## Form A
1. **A.** PBW (female) = 45.5 + 0.91 x (160 - 152.4) = 52.4 kg; 6 x 52.4 = 315 mL. B and C use actual weight; D is 10 mL/kg of actual weight.
2. **A.** High peak with normal plateau = resistive problem. Pneumothorax, mainstem intubation and edema lower compliance, so plateau rises too.
3. **D.** Driving pressure = 26 - 8 = 18; compliance = 420 / 18 = 23 mL/cmH2O. B and A use the plateau or wrong subtraction.
4. **B.** Flow still going out when the next breath starts means air trapping. The other options describe normal features.
5. **C.** Air trapping with reduced venous return. Brief disconnection relieves the trapped air; then slow the rate, allow more expiratory time and treat obstruction. Raising the rate or PEEP worsens trapping; fluid alone does not treat the cause.
6. **C.** Hypoxemia with acceptable plateau and driving pressure: raise PEEP in steps, stopping if plateau or driving pressure rise without oxygenation gain. Lower PEEP worsens shunt; 10 mL/kg violates lung-protective ventilation.
7. **D.** She was compensating with a high minute ventilation. Match it and recheck. Bicarbonate alone does not fix inadequate ventilation.
8. **C.** RSBI = rate / VT(L) = 22 / 0.44 = 50, below about 105, favors success. B is a unit error.
9. **A.** A scooped (concave) inspiratory pressure in volume control means flow is below demand.
10. **B.** Late cycling in obstructed lungs: raise the cycle-off percentage so inspiration ends earlier.

## Form B
1. **B.** PBW (male) = 50 + 0.91 x (180 - 152.4) = 75.1 kg; 6 x 75.1 = 450 mL. A, C, D are 6, 8, 10 mL/kg of actual weight.
2. **C.** Both pressures rose with a normal gap: compliance problem (pneumothorax, mainstem intubation). A, B and D are resistive: peak rises, plateau does not.
3. **A.** Driving pressure = 24 - 10 = 14; compliance = 360 / 14 = 26 mL/cmH2O. B divides by the plateau.
4. **D.** Auto-PEEP = total PEEP - set PEEP = 12 - 5 = 7.
5. **D.** Same principle as Form A item 5.
6. **A.** Prone positioning (PROSEVA) for severe ARDS with P/F below about 150 on FiO2 at least 0.6 and PEEP at least 5. B and C are harmful or unhelpful; sedation interruption does not address refractory hypoxemia.
7. **B.** Winter's formula: 1.5 x 14 + 8 = 29. A PaCO2 of 40 is inadequate compensation here.
8. **A.** RSBI = 30 / 0.25 = 120, above about 105, higher risk of failure.
9. **B.** Neural inspiratory time longer than the ventilator's: the effort continues after cycling and triggers a second breath.
10. **C.** Early cycling: lower the cycle-off percentage so flow must fall further, lengthening the breath.

## Scoring
One point per correct item per form (0 to 10). Confidence items a to f are scored 1 to 5 and reported separately.
