**1.** A man, 180 cm tall and 110 kg, has ARDS. Which tidal volume equals 6 mL/kg of predicted body weight?
A. 660 mL
B. 450 mL
C. 880 mL
D. 1,100 mL

**2.** A patient on volume control has peak pressure rising from 26 to 38 cmH2O, and the plateau rises from 21 to 35 cmH2O. What is the most likely explanation?
A. Mucus plug
B. Kinked endotracheal tube
C. Pneumothorax or right mainstem intubation
D. Bronchospasm

**3.** Tidal volume 360 mL, plateau pressure 24 cmH2O, total PEEP 10 cmH2O. What are the driving pressure and static compliance?
A. 14 cmH2O; 26 mL/cmH2O
B. 24 cmH2O; 15 mL/cmH2O
C. 14 cmH2O; 15 mL/cmH2O
D. 10 cmH2O; 36 mL/cmH2O

**4.** The set PEEP is 5 cmH2O and an expiratory hold reads 12 cmH2O. What is the auto-PEEP?
A. 17 cmH2O
B. 12 cmH2O
C. 5 cmH2O
D. 7 cmH2O

**5.** A 25-year-old with severe asthma is intubated and bagged at about 28 breaths/min. Now MAP is 55, pressures are high and flow does not return to zero between breaths. What is the best immediate action?
A. Increase the rate
B. Increase the tidal volume
C. Give a fluid bolus only and keep the same settings
D. Disconnect briefly to allow exhalation, then lower the rate, allow a longer expiratory time and give bronchodilators

**6.** A patient with ARDS has a P/F ratio of 90 on FiO2 1.0 and PEEP 14, tidal volume 6 mL/kg PBW, plateau 27, driving pressure 13. What intervention has shown a mortality benefit in this situation?
A. Prone positioning for at least 12 to 16 hours a day
B. Lowering PEEP
C. Raising tidal volume
D. Stopping sedation

**7.** A 40-year-old with sepsis and lactic acidosis (HCO3 14 mmol/L) is intubated at rate 12, VT 480. PaCO2 is 40 and pH is 7.16. By Winter's formula the expected compensatory PaCO2 is about 29. What is the best approach?
A. Accept PaCO2 40 as normal
B. Raise minute ventilation toward a PaCO2 of about 29
C. Lower the rate
D. Lower FiO2

**8.** On a spontaneous breathing trial the respiratory rate is 30 and the exhaled tidal volume is 0.25 L. What is the RSBI and what does it suggest?
A. 120, higher risk of failure
B. 7.5, success
C. 120, success
D. 12, failure

**9.** A strong, tachypneic patient on volume control with an inspiratory time of 0.5 s receives two breaths back to back. What is the most likely mechanism?
A. Auto-triggering from a leak
B. The patient's neural inspiratory time is longer than the ventilator's inspiratory time
C. Auto-PEEP from weak efforts
D. PEEP set too high

**10.** On pressure support, the ventilator ends each breath while the patient is still pulling in, giving small breaths followed by a retrigger. Which adjustment helps most?
A. Raise PEEP
B. Raise the cycle-off percentage
C. Lower the cycle-off percentage so flow must fall further
D. Shorten the rise time
